## Self-check per topic

Use the same checklist for every topic; a 15-minute presentation typically fits 1 main theorem with proof + 1–2 corollaries + examples.

1. State the setting and all definitions precisely (spaces, norms, topology).
2. State the main theorem with every hypothesis — and know why each hypothesis is needed (counterexample if dropped).
3. Present the proof at board speed; know which lemmas you'll state without proof.
4. Have **at least one concrete example** ready for each definition.
5. Name 1–2 consequences or applications.
6. Breadth: be able to say in 2–3 sentences what each of the *other* seven topics is about.

## Topic notes

**1 · Hahn–Banach.** Sublinear functionals, real and complex versions, extension with norm preservation; corollaries: functionals separating points, norm via the dual, $X \hookrightarrow X^{**}$ isometric.

**2 · Uniform Boundedness.** Baire category argument; Banach–Steinhaus; pointwise-bounded families of operators on a Banach space are uniformly bounded. Typical application examples from Lecture 3.

**3 · Inverse Mapping / Closed Graph.** Open Mapping Theorem via Baire; bounded inverse of a bijective bounded operator; graph-closed ⇒ bounded between Banach spaces. Know why completeness is essential.

**4 · Finite-dimensional norms.** All norms equivalent; consequences: finite-dimensional subspaces are closed, linear maps from finite-dimensional spaces are bounded, closed unit ball compact ⇔ finite dimension (Riesz).

**5 · Banach–Alaoglu.** Weak and weak\* topologies, nets; closed unit ball of $X^*$ is weak\*-compact (Tychonoff argument).

**6 · Arzelà–Ascoli.** Equicontinuity, uniform equicontinuity, total boundedness; relative compactness in $C(K)$ for compact metric $K$.

**7 · Compact operators.** Definition, ideal property, closedness in $B(X,Y)$, adjoint of compact is compact, limits of finite-rank operators (on Hilbert space).

**8 · Spectral Theorem.** Self-adjoint compact operators on Hilbert space: eigenvalues real, eigenvectors for distinct eigenvalues orthogonal, eigenvalue set countable with 0 as only possible accumulation point, orthonormal eigenbasis decomposition.
